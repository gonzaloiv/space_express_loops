# Later chapters — after the instrument

**Status:** idea · cross-area · updated 2026-11-26 — chapters 4–6 and other ways to grow the game after
the store release; [chapters](../design/chapters.md) owns chapters 1–3 and the freelance contracts.

## Chapters 4–6 — delivery rules

Each adds **one requirement on the delivery**, not new physics: the cube and gravity stay; what counts
as a good arrival changes. A new rule costs one bake check and one field cue. Same shape as the
designed chapters: ~2 teaching jobs + 6–8 jobs that use it.

| Ch | Theme | New rule | Field cue | Lore hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Time** | arrival window (too fast or too slow fails) | window marker at the destination | the release ending's slogan: "Today the space. Tomorrow the time." |
| 5 | **Fragile cargo** | a limit on how sharply the path bends or how fast it goes | stress glow on the ship | the cake, the live animal, the piano — the cargo finally matters |
| 6 | **Stops** | pass near a waypoint planet; avoid no-fly zones | ring on the waypoint, tinted no-fly volume | deliveries with pickups; client callbacks |

Planets on orbits would fit Time, but a moving well breaks the static field the flight is integrated
over ([field elements](element-pack.md) §Rules): that is new physics, not a delivery rule, so it is
its own decision.

Six chapters of 8–10 jobs (~45–55 designed jobs) is the size for a ~€10–15 game.

## Broken physics — fixing the universe

The story's long arc ([lore](../design/world/lore.md) §Episodes): after something breaks, the job
turns from delivering cargo to repairing spacetime with the Wrap.

- **The first broken bodies are in the store release** (2026-11-25). Chapter 3 presents the scramble
  as damage and teaches anchored planets and repulsors ([chapters](../design/chapters.md) §Chapter 3).
  What follows builds on the same idea: every body has its own response to the Wrap.
- **Debris.** A broken planet: a tight cluster of small fragments, closer together than the cube's
  smallest size, so the player cannot edit them one at a time. Each fragment is its own kind (normal,
  anchored, repulsor), so one pinch reshapes the cluster unevenly and the player reads which fragments
  matter. Costs: bake time (more sources; treat the cluster as one zone candidate), a cue that reads at
  fragment size, and lattice/frame cost on the Quest.
- **Inverted bodies.** A body that does the opposite of the pinch: it grows when you shrink. Hard to
  read at a glance; add it only if a playtest of the broken bodies asks for more.
- **Other broken laws.** A well that bends the wrong way, a tear the ship cannot cross. Each needs new
  physics, a field cue and bake support.

## Other options

- **Sold stars — dark systems on purpose.** The firm sells off stars (Slightly Used Stars Ltd. is a
  star dealer); from one job on, a system with no sun means "this one's star was sold". Needs a line or
  card at the first dark job, a deliberate starless node on the map (a burnt-out remnant marker, not
  empty space: the map anchors every job on its sun today), and a rule for which later jobs stay dark.
  For the store release every system keeps a star (decided 2026-11-26); this could carry chapter 3's
  damage or a later episode.
- **Ghost of your best path.** A faint line of your previous best trajectory on retry; makes chasing
  3★ readable.
- **Hard versions of solved jobs.** Same field, budget at 1.5× the solution instead of 3×. It breaks
  the "never tighten the budget" rule, so it is opt-in only.
- **Player-made contracts.** A player creator and seed-code sharing. Needs a lighter solvability check
  than the editor bake; far future.
