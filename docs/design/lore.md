# Lore — "Our Space"

**Status:** design flavour, not a plan. The fiction that frames the transport-company
pivot in [project direction](project-direction.md) and the resource model in
[transport company](transport-company.md).
**Date:** 2026-10-23.

## 0. Verdict

The lore is a **frame**, and its one job is to **justify the loop**: it explains why
the only lever is *cost*. Keep it as backdrop and subtext — never as exposition.

## 1. The setting (2048)

- A **stationary-state society**: no births, no new markets, no growth. Population
  flat or declining.
- The economy is a **fixed pie**. Companies do not grow; they survive by being
  cheaper.
- **Fuel is expensive.** Transport is a pure cost centre.
- Trade still exists — goods still move between settlements. Only the *market* is
  fixed. Nobody needs more customers; everybody needs lower cost.

## 2. The tech: spacetime wrapping

- A **new system** built by a tech team to manage transport: **wrapping spacetime**.
- In-fiction product name: **the Wrap** (one term). The *action* stays "shaping" —
  the word the design docs use.
- It is **new and experimental**, which justifies why the player is learning it and
  why it *is* the game.
- **Why it exists.** The world finally put a **hard cap on fuel** (not like today), so the company
  could not burn more — and found a way to spend **entropy** directly instead. It is untested and,
  in principle, can **literally destroy the universe**. Ironic, given today's situation.

## 3. "Revenue is relative"

The tagline. Three readings stack:

1. **Fixed pie** — revenue is a *share*, not a total. You don't grow it; you keep
   more of it by spending less.
2. **Frame-dependent** — value is measured against a clock, and clocks are
   frame-dependent. The same delivery is worth different amounts depending on whose
   time you settle it in.
3. **The physics name.**

The payoff is (3): the fiction's economics and the mechanic's physics are the **same
idea**.

**How to use it:** as an *in-world* slogan — a poster the tech team hung in the
office, which the characters roll their eyes at. **Not** as the pitch line: the double
meaning only lands once the player already knows the game is about relativity.
Self-aware puns land; sincere ones don't (the same instinct that killed the "Shape the
Space" subtitle).

## 4. Greed is eco-friendly

The theme. Normally eco and profit pull apart and you build a choice around the gap.
Here the physics **collapses** them: a gravity assist is *literally* getting something
for nothing by reading the field better. The greedy play **is** the green play. No
scold, no guilt, no "green mode."

- **No dilemma is the feature** — but know where the fault line is. In the model it
  already exists: **entropy-greed vs the time promise.** Editing little is cheap, and
  the victim is the customer's window. That is the real tension
  ([transport company](transport-company.md) §2).
- **Darker, later (optional):** the cheap path grazes someone's home, or parks the
  crew in a time well — a social or human cost, externalised off the ledger.
  "Eco-friendly greed" is only honest while the ledger is the whole world.

## 5. The bleak reading (keep it ambiguous)

Society only gets greener because it **stopped growing and got desperate** —
scarcity, not virtue. Let the player read it as triumph *or* satire. Do not resolve it
in dialogue; the ambiguity is what makes a small game stick.

## 6. Guardrails

- **Backdrop, not exposition.** Nobody says "revenue is relative" or "greed is
  eco-friendly" out loud. An entropy-saved number climbing while margin climbs *is* the
  statement.
- **One term.** The system is "the Wrap"; the verb is "shaping".
- **Plain language** for the player: "the ship's clock" and "entropy" (the bar); "eco rating" is
  the flavour, not a second term. No other physics jargon.
- **Tone: melancholy and adult** — a company that survives, not one that conquers.
  Not a growth fantasy.
- **Don't stack puns in the pitch.** One signature pun ("Our Space") plus one
  in-world slogan is enough.

## 7. Seeds (later, not the test slice)

- **Make the pun mechanically true.** Settle some contracts in the ship's
  proper-time frame and others in Earth's, so a job's margin literally depends on the
  clock. That turns "revenue is relative" from flavour into a rule.
- **Externalised costs.** A cheap route that grazes an inhabited world, or a crew put
  in a time well, as a social/human price hidden from the ledger.
- **The tech team as characters.** They built the Wrap and roll their eyes at the
  slogan — a way to voice the satire without preaching.

## 8. Open questions

1. **Why is the population static?** Post-scarcity, demographic collapse, or a
   deliberate limit? Pick one; don't explain it in-game.
2. **Is the player the company, or a dispatcher inside it?** Affects voice and framing.
3. **Does the Wrap replace the old system, or run beside it?** "New and experimental"
   suggests replacing, with the old way as the fallback/baseline.
4. **How bleak?** Triumph reading, satire reading, or both left open (recommended:
   both).
