# Lore — "Our Space"

**Status:** design · world · lore.
**Date:** 2026-10-23.

**Related:** [vision](../vision.md) · [resources](../systems/resources.md) ·
[progression](../systems/progression.md) · [glossary](../glossary.md)

## Goal

The fiction is a **frame**, and its one job is to **justify the loop**: it explains why the only
lever is *cost*. Keep it as backdrop and subtext — never as exposition. Money is **flavour only**
([resources](../systems/resources.md)).

Its voice is **Jim's** — the CEO, who operates the Wrap himself. Everything here is what Jim knows and
would say, not narration.

## The setting (2048)

- A **stationary-state society**: no births, no new markets, no growth. Population flat or declining.
- **The decline is demographic collapse, not policy.** Nobody chose to stop growing — it happened. The
  fixed pie is a symptom, not a decision.
- The economy is a **fixed pie**. Companies do not grow; they survive by being cheaper.
- **Fuel is expensive.** Transport is a pure cost centre.
- Trade still exists — goods still move between settlements. Only the *market* is fixed. Nobody
  needs more customers; everybody needs lower cost.

## Who you are — Jim

- **Jim runs the company.** A small firm; he is the **CEO** and the **dispatcher** — he works the Wrap
  himself, because there is no one else.
- **The Wrap is not his idea.** He is not an enthusiast — he is a man doing his job with a machine he
  does not fully trust.
- **The voice is his.** Dry, tired, adult; understatement, not exclamation. He never explains the
  physics — he complains about it.

## The tech: spacetime wrapping

- A **new system** built by a tech team to manage transport: **wrapping spacetime** — the **Wrap**
  ([glossary](../glossary.md)).
- It is **new and experimental**, which justifies why the player is learning it and why it *is* the
  game.
- **Why it exists.** The world put a **hard cap on fuel**, so the company could not burn more — and
  found a way to spend **entropy** directly instead. It is untested and, in principle, can
  **literally destroy the universe**.
- **It is the company's new plan.** In a fixed pie, the Wrap is how the firm means to make more money —
  and **Jim is the one who has to run it**, ready or not.

## The progression (the frame for the run)

The run is the company **proving the Wrap**, one delivery at a time. All backdrop:

- **The map is the contracts.** Each **numbered cube** is a prepared delivery; the number is the tech
  team's order of readiness, not a menu. Picking a cube brings its patch of spacetime into the room
  ([progression](../systems/progression.md)).
- **The order is calibration.** The Wrap is new, so it is proven **step by step** — each delivery that
  lands widens what the company will wrap next (more bodies, tighter promises). What the player feels as
  difficulty is the tech team's growing nerve. Nothing else gates the run: no credits, no rank.
- **The stars are the margin.** The eco rating is the **entropy** the delivery did *not* spend. A firm
  that survives by being cheaper lives on that number; a three-star job is one the ledger is proud of.
- **The map fills with the network.** A solved cube **lights**, and the lit map *is* the company's
  reach — a small firm quietly covering more of the sector. That filling is the progression feel.

The **window** is the customer's promise — the one thing the company must not break
([resources](../systems/resources.md)). Editing little and arriving on time are the same play; the stars
are where it shows up as a number.

## The customers

- The other end of every window is a **client** — a settlement or firm in the same stationary economy.
- **Names are absurd on purpose** (random, comic): the sector is bleak, the paperwork is not. It keeps
  the tone from tipping into misery.
- The client is **never a face** — a name, a window, a promise.

## The tech team

- **One voice.** The team that built the Wrap is heard as **a single character** who talks — with voice
  — through the run. Not a cast; a colleague.
- **Anonymous tip panels** carry the rest: short, in-world hints when the player needs them,
  unattributed and diegetic (a console readout, not a tutorial box).

## "Revenue is relative"

The tagline. Three readings stack:

1. **Fixed pie** — revenue is a *share*, not a total. You keep more of it by spending less.
2. **Frame-dependent** — value is measured against a clock, and clocks are frame-dependent. The
   same delivery is worth different amounts depending on whose time you settle it in.
3. **The physics name.**

The payoff is (3): the fiction's economics and the mechanic's physics are the **same idea**.

**How to use it:** as an *in-world* slogan — a poster the tech team hung, which Jim rolls his eyes at.
**Not** as the pitch line: the double meaning only lands once the player knows the game is about
relativity.

## Greed is eco-friendly

The theme. Normally eco and profit pull apart and you build a choice around the gap. Here the
physics **collapses** them: a gravity assist is *literally* getting something for nothing by
reading the field better. The greedy play **is** the green play. No scold, no guilt.

- **No dilemma is the feature** — but know where the fault line is. It already exists:
  **entropy-greed vs the time promise.** Editing little is cheap, and the victim is the customer's
  window ([resources](../systems/resources.md)).
- **Darker, later (optional):** the cheap path grazes someone's home, or parks the crew in a time
  well — a social or human cost, externalised off the ledger.

## The bleak reading (keep it ambiguous)

Society only gets greener because it **stopped growing and got desperate** — scarcity, not virtue.
Let the player read it as triumph *or* satire. Do not resolve it in dialogue; the ambiguity is what
makes a small game stick.

## Guardrails

- **Backdrop, not exposition.** Nobody says "revenue is relative" out loud. An entropy-saved number
  climbing while margin climbs *is* the statement.
- **One term per idea** — the fixed terms are in the [glossary](../glossary.md). No other physics
  jargon.
- **Tone: melancholy and adult** — a company that survives, not one that conquers.
- **Don't stack puns in the pitch.** One signature pun ("Our Space") plus one in-world slogan.

## Seeds (later)

- **Make the pun mechanically true.** Settle some contracts in the ship's proper-time frame and
  others in Earth's, so a job's margin literally depends on the clock.
- **Externalised costs.** A cheap route that grazes an inhabited world, or a crew put in a time
  well, as a social/human price hidden from the ledger.
- **The tech voice as a person.** Give the single character an arc across the run — pride, doubt, and the
  thing they will not say about the Wrap.
- **Per-job launch (the narrative fuel).** Once the launch is per-job, each job's *given* launch
  becomes a story beat — a launch that would fail, made to land.
- **The lit map in the office.** The map as a physical object the firm lives around — lit cubes as quiet
  pride, dark ones as the work left.

## Canon (settled)

- **Static because demographic collapse** — nobody chose it; the fixed pie is a symptom.
- **Jim, the CEO**, is the player and the voice; he runs the Wrap himself, in a small firm.
- **The Wrap is the company's new plan** to make more money; Jim is the one forced to use it.
- **Clients have absurd names**; the client is never a face.
- **The tech team is one voiced character**, with anonymous tip panels for hints.
- **The reading stays ambiguous** — triumph or satire, unresolved.
